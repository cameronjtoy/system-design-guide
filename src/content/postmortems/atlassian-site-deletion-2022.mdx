## What happened

Atlassian was retiring a legacy standalone app, "Insight – Asset Management", whose functionality had moved into Jira Service Management. The plan was to delete the old app from the customer sites where it was still installed.

The team responsible for the deletion received a list of IDs to remove. But the list contained the IDs of the **customers' entire cloud sites**, not the IDs of the app installations. The deletion script used an API that accepted either kind of ID, and it ran in a mode that **permanently deleted** data rather than marking it for deletion.

Between 07:38 and 08:01 UTC on April 5, the script deleted **883 sites belonging to 775 customers**.

## Why restoration took up to two weeks

Atlassian had backups and could restore any single site within its recovery objectives. What it didn't have was an **automated process to restore hundreds of sites at once**, each with several products, linked data and third-party apps, back into a multi-tenant environment where other customers' data now occupied the space.

- Early restorations were largely manual and took days per batch.
- Midway through, the team built a new, more automated restoration approach, which sped up the later batches.
- Communication suffered: deleted sites also removed the contact information Atlassian needed to reach affected administrators, and customers felt updates were slow.

All sites were restored by April 18, with at most five minutes of data loss because backups and point-in-time recovery worked as designed.

## What changed

- **Soft deletes everywhere:** deletion now marks data for removal and keeps it recoverable for a period, rather than deleting immediately. Permanent deletion requires a separate, gated step.
- **Safer APIs:** destructive APIs no longer accept ambiguous IDs, and scripts must validate what an ID refers to before acting.
- **Staged execution for bulk operations:** run on a small batch first and verify before continuing.
- **Automated, tested multi-site restoration** so large-scale restores can happen in hours.
- Improved incident communication, including keeping customer contacts available outside the affected systems.

## Lessons

<Callout type="tip" title="Key takeaways">
- **Make deletion reversible by default.** Soft delete with a retention period turns a catastrophe into an undo.
- **Ambiguous IDs are a trap.** An API that accepts "a site or an app" by the same kind of ID invites exactly this mistake. Use typed IDs and validate them.
- **Treat bulk scripts like deploys.** Canary them on a few targets, check the result, then proceed.
- **Test restoring at scale, not just restoring one thing.** The backups were fine; the process to use them for hundreds of customers at once didn't exist.
</Callout>
