## The problem it solves

Billing totals off by a cent, meetings shifting by an hour after daylight saving time, a platform outage on February 29th, names that won't save because of an accent. These bugs come from choosing the wrong data type on day one, and they are painful to fix once data is stored.

## How it works

### Time

There are two different kinds of time, and they need different storage:

| Kind | Example | Store as |
|---|---|---|
| **An instant** (something happened) | Order placed, message sent | UTC timestamp (`timestamptz` in Postgres) |
| **A future local event** (wall-clock time) | "Dentist at 9:00 in Berlin on March 30" | Local date-time **plus** the IANA time zone (`Europe/Berlin`) |

Why the difference: time zone rules change. If you convert a future 9:00 meeting to UTC today and the government changes daylight saving rules, the stored UTC value now means 8:00 or 10:00. Store the user's intent and convert at display time.

Other rules:

- Store and compare in UTC; convert to local time only in the UI.
- A **date** (birthday, due date) is not a timestamp; storing it as midnight UTC shows it as the previous day for users west of UTC.
- Never do calendar math by hand ("add 1 to the year", "add 86,400 seconds for a day"); use a date library that knows about leap years and daylight saving.
- Server clocks drift; don't use timestamps from different machines to order events precisely. See [clocks and ordering](/principles/clocks-and-ordering).

### Money

- **Never use floating point.** `0.1 + 0.2` is not `0.3` in binary floating point.
- Store **integer minor units** (cents) or a fixed-precision decimal, **plus a currency code** (ISO 4217, e.g. `USD`, `JPY`). Currencies have different numbers of decimal places: yen has none.
- Decide and document rounding rules (per line item or per invoice?), especially for tax and currency conversion.
- Store the exchange rate used with any converted amount; rates change.

### Text

- Use UTF-8 everywhere, including the database, connection and API. Names, addresses and messages contain accents, non-Latin scripts and emoji.
- Don't impose Western name assumptions ("first name" and "last name" are required, names have no spaces).
- Compare emails and usernames case-insensitively, consistently (a normalized column or a case-insensitive collation and unique index).
- Length limits should count characters the way users see them, and the database's limit should match the UI's.

### Enums and status fields

- Store statuses as constrained values (an enum type or a `CHECK` constraint), not free text.
- Expect new values: clients and downstream consumers must handle a status they don't recognize.

## Trade-offs

- **Integers vs decimals for money:** integers are fast and exact but need care with currency exponents; decimals are clearer but slower and need fixed precision.
- **Database enums vs check constraints vs lookup tables:** native enums are compact but awkward to change in some databases; a lookup table is most flexible.
- **Normalizing text:** lowercasing or stripping accents helps matching but loses the original; store both if you need both.

## In the real world

<Callout type="warning" title="Azure's leap day outage">
On February 29, 2012, Azure code generated certificates valid "until the same date next year" by adding one to the year, producing February 29, 2013, a date that doesn't exist. Certificate creation failed, new virtual machines couldn't start, and the failures cascaded across the platform. See [Azure's leap day bug](/postmortems/azure-leap-day-2012).
</Callout>

The PostgreSQL community's "Don't Do This" wiki page lists many of these traps directly: don't use `timestamp` without time zone to store instants, don't use the `money` type, and don't use floating point for currency.

## Interview angle

<Callout type="interview">
When your design stores times or money, say how: "timestamps in UTC; scheduled events as local time plus time zone; amounts as integer cents with a currency code." It's a small detail that signals real-world experience.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="For PMs">
Specify time zone behavior in requirements ("reminders fire at 9:00 in the user's current time zone" vs "in the time zone where it was created") and rounding rules for money with finance before launch.
</Callout>
